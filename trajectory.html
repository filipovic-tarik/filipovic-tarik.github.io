Orbital-plane close-up · 60.3°

The ecliptic and Galactic planes

The amber Galactic plane is the imaginary plane enclosed by the Sun’s orbit around the Milky Way. The blue ecliptic plane is enclosed by Earth’s orbit around the Sun. They intersect at 60.3° - not at the perpendicular 90° angle that might seem intuitive. The model therefore tilts the initially upright Earth-orbit loop by the complementary 29.7°.

Galactic orbit radius · R

26,000 light-years from the Galactic center

The white radius joins Sagittarius A* at the center of the Milky Way to the Sun’s current position. In the model, this distance is approximately 2.5 × 10²⁰ meters.

Earth’s orbital extremes

Periapsis and apoapsis

Periapsis is Earth’s nearest point to the Sun, at 1.47098074 × 10¹¹ meters. Apoapsis is its farthest point, at 1.52097701 × 10¹¹ meters. The small difference between them gives Earth’s orbit its slight eccentricity.
